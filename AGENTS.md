## Product boundary

T.O.O.N. is a historical creative-studio management game about building one eccentric company across roughly 1900–2030. The studio, recurring professionals, persistent IPs, relationships, physical workplace, historical context, productions and archive evolve together across changing media.

Canonical production core:

`IP -> Production -> Medium -> Crew -> Stages -> Release -> Reception -> Legacy`

Media-specific structures such as panels, storyboard/shots or 3D asset stages are bounded submodels. Do not promote any one medium's pipeline into the permanent game-wide hierarchy.

Implement only the subset needed for the current proof.

## First onboarding progression — owner accepted (2026-11-17)

1. **Guided external comic-strip commission:** the story introduces a client-owned IP and an explicit external brief. The player learns to accept the contract, carry the physical production between stations, complete the 3–4-panel strip and personally authorize delivery at the physical publishing/release endpoint. Under G-11/G-12, there is **no advance**, guaranteed payment is due **on delivery**, and there is **no client approval/rejection gate** afterward; brief/quality affects reputation and future offers rather than guaranteed payment. The IP stays client-owned under G-13. The onboarding should not introduce Marketing or interpersonal conflict as a prerequisite to completing the first paid job.
2. **Player-original comic strip:** the player originates the next strip's **own concept** (with the G-03 permitted concept-level editing/AI assistance) and repeats the **same physical production route**, with reduced guidance. This production belongs to the studio's original-IP flow, **not the external client's IP**; commercial returns are governed by G-14 rather than G-11 contractual delivery. The player still explicitly authorizes publication (G-08).
3. **More strip commissions → studio growth → first manageable conflict:** further requests for tirinhas create a visible reason to expand studio capacity, learn a small staffing/expansion action, and then encounter a **low-stakes, clearly resolvable conflict between recurring professionals**. Introduce the decision/relation consequence **here**, not before the first two full production cycles. The actual story trigger, characters, options, numbers, building expansion and persistence proof remain unspecified; do not invent an entire conflict subsystem or resolve G-57's previously offered alternatives by assumption.

**Proof checkpoints:** finish/deliver one client strip; finish/publish one player-original strip; expose new strip workload and basic studio expansion; present/resolve one simple people Decision Event with an observable persistent effect. This revises the previous all-at-once first vertical-slice fixture into **three sequential validation milestones**. 3–4 panels are a target, not a hard validity gate. No Marketing in the strip-only phase; player transports work manually in this initial sequence; keep G-07 stage closure and G-08 final authority. **Accepted direction / Documented, not Unreal implementation evidence.**

## Structural design closure — organic progression and minimal production defaults (2026-11-17)

**Owner accepted A — organic campaign progression:** after the explicitly staged first two strips and first growth/conflict tutorial, studio evolution is driven by **player choices responding to available opportunities, historically plausible media/capabilities and actual studio resources**, rather than an obligatory mission chain or fixed reputation-level gates. Historical context constrains what can exist, not when the player must pursue it. A new production medium requires compatible workstations, people and resources to use; old media remain viable. Do not invent a parallel unlock currency, mandatory prestige threshold or fixed era-end mission. Earlier G-24 professionalization and G-26 Marketing-after-strip-only constraints remain in force; their precise activation moment is content/pacing to validate.

**Recommended minimum rule set for remaining structural gaps — proposed defaults, not separately owner-ratified mechanics:**
- **Player agency / meaningful actions:** each contract/production presents at least one existing meaningful choice among opportunity, staff assignment/priorities, bounded concept/script/art-direction changes, whether to request another version, spending/time or publication. Physical transport communicates state and costs attention but is **not the sole source of strategic depth**. Do not add a minigame for every station.
- **Quality and outcomes:** retain the current **hybrid reception** based on actual artifact evidence plus game-owned process/people/brief/context data; uncertainty remains explicit. Decisions affect the work and available iterations where feasible. AI evaluation offers structured evidence, **never directly modifies authoritative reputation, contract payment or revenue**. Existing G-11–G-15 economic, rights and reputation rules own the outcome. Use a few readable factors initially; no universal opaque single quality score or full balance table needed for design freeze.
- **Studio capacity:** existing compatible workstation slots, finite professional time, cash and deadlines create the reason to expand when extra strip demand arrives. Use the existing build/hire/assignment framework; do not introduce a second staffing-capacity or mandatory expansion-mission system. The first onboarding expansion can be a small capacity increase, not a complete construction economy.
- **People / conflicts:** use existing deterministic, **authored parameterized Decision Events**, relationship state and visible consequences. For the first conflict, one low-stakes disagreement, two understandable response choices and a bounded persistent relationship/workflow consequence suffice. Do not require advanced autonomous-dialogue or new character subsystems to prove this beat.
- **Pacing:** retain a continuous clock, pause for consequential decisions/build mode, manual acceleration and meaningful automatic downtime compression, the **provisional** 1900–2030/~40-hour target, and explicit playtest tuning. No fixed per-era quota, invented mandated production count or new time system.

**Closure boundary:** owner approved only the **organic progression model** in this turn; the five remaining bullets are **default validation baselines**, suitable for preproduction planning but not independent immutable owner decisions. Proposed **design-freeze candidate** pending checks of meaningful first-strip choices, cause/effect visibility, early staffing capacity and expected pacing; detailed values, era transition thresholds, asset schemas, AI performance, Unreal implementation and narrative are content/engineering/playtest work, not repeated owner design votes. Continue only for demonstrated contradictions or decisions that materially affect the player-facing loop.

## Research Department — mandatory technology research (owner accepted option A, 2026-11-17)

**Accepted direction / Documented:** every **new technology for the studio** requires **completed Research Department R&D before the studio can adopt/use it**, even when it has already become commonplace in the historical world. Calendar date alone never grants the studio a new technology. Historical era/world availability still constrains *what may be researched*; R&D does not invent anachronistic world technology. Once researched, actual utilization still requires relevant compatible professional skills, facilities/equipment, resources and any existing production conditions. Research is **studio-level technological capability**, not tied to an individual finished production or the unrelated G-35–G-42 **Marketing audience research**.

**Preserve organic progression:** after the staged comic-strip onboarding, the player chooses which eligible technological capabilities to research, and when, according to opportunity/cost; no mandatory narrative mission chain or abstract studio-level gate. Existing production types and already acquired capabilities remain usable; advancing the calendar does not revoke them.

**Initial R&D baseline — owner accepted option A (2026-11-17):** the starting studio already possesses the **baseline technological know-how required for the first commissioned and player-original comic strips**, treated as its established starting capability rather than forcing the player to perform or pay for retrospective research. The **Research Department is not built or required for the first strip tutorial** and may be constructed/introduced later when meaningful new historically eligible technologies create an opportunity. This is an **initial-condition exception**, not an exemption for *new* post-start technologies: every such technology still needs completed R&D before studio use. Do not invent exact starting tech IDs, first department opening date, or additional free unlocks. **Research scope — owner accepted option B (2026-11-17):** R&D unlocks **new technological capabilities**: meaningful production techniques, processes, specialized tool capabilities and significant technological upgrades. It does **not** require researching each product model, duplicate unit, ordinary replacement or extra compatible workstation/room capacity using a capability the studio already knows. Research a new capability **once at studio scope**; afterward the player can buy/build compatible equipment and increase capacity under existing cost, staffing and space constraints. Historically eligible capabilities still require R&D even if already commonplace in the world; basic comic-strip capabilities are starting knowledge under the owner-approved exception. Distinguish new functional capabilities from superficial equipment SKUs rather than inventing a technology entry for every purchase. **Research dependencies — owner accepted option C (2026-11-17):** technological capabilities belong to a **structured research tree with branches and multi-step prerequisite chains**. A node becomes researchable only when historically eligible **and** its required predecessor capabilities are already researched at studio scope. This is a genuine designed progression tree, not a flat list or incidental minor dependencies; it must still represent **functional capabilities**, not per-product SKUs, and should provide meaningful player choice among eligible branches. Seed the initial comic-strip baseline as already known so tutorial production does not deadlock. Research grants knowledge, not free equipment, staff or production. **Do not invent tree nodes, specific branch names, dates, unlock order or research durations until authored and validated.** **Research execution — owner accepted option B (2026-11-17):** **existing studio professionals**, rather than a mandatory specialist researcher profession, are assigned from their ordinary production work to advance chosen eligible technology-tree research. Research consumes their **finite working time and studio resources**, competing with production assignments, deadlines and cash. During time actually assigned to R&D, the same worker **cannot simultaneously contribute that time to normal production**; switching assignments remains a management action under the existing staff/priorities model and must not duplicate capacity or progress. R&D does not advance from money alone or merely from the calendar while nobody does research. The Research Department is the organizational owner of technology projects, not a separate compulsory pool of specialist hires. The detailed physical workspace requirement, whether/how work is split among employees, task scheduling, skill-affinity impacts, rates and prices are **implementation/balance constraints**, not extra accepted mechanics. **Structural R&D design closed for preproduction:** mandatory capability R&D, initial known strip baseline, branched prerequisite tree and shared existing workforce are now accepted. Remain explicit that tree contents, department availability, staffing/job UX and validation are **not implemented**.

## Human Resources Department — integrated HR direction (owner accepted C, 2026-11-18)

**Accepted direction / Documented:** T.O.O.N. includes an **integrated Human Resources (RH) Department** combining **professional development, people-management support, organizational culture, workplace wellbeing and environment/decor initiatives**. Its purpose is to create actionable, visible **interventions on existing studio people, relationships and physical-environment systems**, not to duplicate hiring, dismissal, assignment, direct management conversations, construction, AI character cognition or Decision Events. A decoration may affect different employees differently according to existing personality/preferences, relationships and context; RH must not be a guaranteed '+happiness' shop or erase social conflict with a button.

**Authority boundary:** the player remains responsible for managerial decisions and the physical purchase/placement of furniture; RH may **propose, organize, unlock/commission or customize** workplace initiatives and related decorative possibilities, with consequences resolved by game-owned state/rules. RH does **not** own asset-art generation or replace the construction system. Examples (design candidates, **not independently owner-approved initiatives**): mentoring, professional development, onboarding, recognition, mediated discussions, rest/social-workspace improvements and culture events. Each should have an intelligible cost/opportunity cost and potentially different results by character, avoiding flat universal bonuses.

**RH execution — owner accepted option B (2026-11-18):** the player **assigns existing studio employees** to execute HR initiatives. This uses their **finite working time and studio resources**, competing with production or research responsibilities; the same worker-hours must never simultaneously count toward an RH initiative and normal production/R&D. There is **no mandatory dedicated HR profession** and no passive investment-only execution shortcut. RH organizes the interventions; existing employee assignments, priority and availability rules remain authoritative. A participant's exact allocation, competency effects, program duration and UI are implementation/balancing, not newly approved standalone mechanics. **HR availability — owner accepted demand-driven technology research (2026-11-18):** as people-management demands arise through studio growth or observable staff/culture problems, the Research Department's **organizational/management capability research** becomes a relevant eligible branch/node in the **existing structured technology tree**. The player uses the existing workforce/R&D flow to complete the research, **then may establish the RH Department**, subject to normal space/resource requirements. **Demand motivates/reveals the opportunity; successful research is the unlock; construction/adoption remains a player choice.** No separate RH unlock currency or calendar-only grant. This is a **management-process technological capability**, not a new device SKU, and it must not create a dependency cycle: research occurs through the already established Research Department, **never RH itself**. Historical eligibility and any authored prerequisite nodes still apply; exact demand signals, technology node placement, first opportunity timing and costs are content/balancing/prototype matters, not owner-approved specifics. Direct hiring/dismissal/conversations, baseline construction and ordinary decor **remain available without RH**. Do not force HR/R&D into the opening two strips. **Department structural design: Accepted direction / Documented; no remaining indispensable conceptual gap identified.** Exact initiatives/decor catalogue, work time, setup costs, UI and tests remain content/implementation/balancing/validation, not Unreal gameplay proof.

## Character names as cultural easter eggs — owner direction (2026-11-18)

**Accepted direction / Documented:** generated character **given names and surnames** draw from a **curated pool of names of notable artists, comics creators, directors and illustrators**, recombined into fictional identities. When a generated **full given-name + surname** combination matches a documented real creative professional, the fictional character gains a **small special gameplay bonus** and may trigger a corresponding discoverable achievement/easter egg. If **two matching full-name fictional characters** in the same studio correspond to a **well-sourced historical creative partnership or documented dispute**, their co-presence can trigger an additional **bounded positive/negative gameplay effect and player achievement**. These are deliberate rare systemic-cultural discoveries, **not fictional copies, personality claims, biographical reenactment, or endorsements by real people**. The effect should sit in existing staff/relationship/collaboration systems and be visibly attributable; it must not overwrite established fictional character personalities/relationships or determine inevitable hostility/friendship. A positive pairing can be a potential affinity; a negative pairing must not force deterministic abuse/harassment. **Historical claims must be documented by credible sources** and especially disputes must avoid unverified gossip/defamatory claims. Distinguish the actual documented relation from merely having similar names.

**Design boundaries:** a curated, licensable/appropriate onomastic content list, verified full-name-match and historically verified pair IDs, explicit bounded reward effects and one-time achievement eligibility. Preserve consistent Unicode/name normalization and persistent identity across save/reload without creating duplicate rewards. Exact names, source evidence, bonus magnitude, pair-event triggers, achievements, gameplay tuning and publicity/legal clearance are **content, implementation, legal review and validation**, not asserted complete. Do not imply real professionals appear as characters in-game. No Unreal implementation or tests performed.

## Studio/cast rules

- Studio professionals are recurring characters, not interchangeable worker resources.
- The cast may include humans, anthropomorphic animals, creatures, living objects and other original cartoon archetypes.
- Appearance/species is separate from personality and from longevity/existence model.
- Characters need readable silhouettes, profession, personality, history and relationship promise.
- Avoid one-note stereotypes.
- Prefer bold macro-shapes, strong line of action, readable posing, and animation-friendly design.
- Do not copy protected Disney, Warner/Looney Tunes, Fleischer, or other studio/character identities.

## G-03 concept, authorship and physical workflow (accepted 2026-11-16)

- Concept may arrive partly populated from an **external contract** (only terms/fields explicitly allowed by the offer are editable), a **single-originator employee pitch**, or **player initiative** (optionally prefilled or AI-assisted).
- **Only concept, scriptwriting and art direction** expose direct player editable creative content. Scriptwriting normally creates a short automatic script but can be player-edited; art direction normally generates automatically and permits reference/image uploads.
- **No direct player text entry or bespoke content-upload editor at storyboard/layout, production, finishing or other later stages**. All generating stages still support requesting another version (maximum three successful versions total for each stage).
- After a valid result, the production **physical object returns to the player**. The player places it at the **next compatible workstation** to advance the chosen/default version; the game does not auto-route between stations. An unavailable/incompatible station is a blocker, not an implicit stage skip.
- Content creation may be automatic without player typing/editing; **for the first vertical slice, station-to-station handoffs remain player management actions**. **G-18 accepted future progression (2026-11-17):** hired **Production Assistants (Assistentes de Produção)** autonomously monitor all eligible studio projects and physically route them between compatible workstations, consuming worker time/capacity; direct manual transport remains the fastest individual route. The first vertical slice still requires manual handoffs. Assistants cannot make player-owned creative/management decisions, request new creative versions, discard projects, override blocked/incompatible stages or authorize publication. This is **not** approval to automate current first-slice handoffs or remove final player authority. Final release still requires player authority. The precise interaction affordance and art-direction field schema remain UX/implementation decisions, not evidence of running software.

- **G-19 accepted (2026-11-17):** when two or more productions become ready for transport, **each Production Assistant** evaluates the eligible waiting production objects using a combination of **(1) physical distance from that assistant to the production, (2) the production's remaining contractual deadline/urgency where applicable, and (3) that assistant's relationship with the professional who LAST worked on the production at its preceding station**. These three factors together determine which production that assistant carries first; **deadline is not an absolute top priority** and relationship is not with the professional at the destination station. Only Production Assistants perform automated transport; manual moves remain player actions. Exact weights, no-deadline handling and tie/fairness policy require balancing. Respect G-18 eligibility, player decisions and G-08 release gate.

- **G-20 accepted (2026-11-17):** for automated Production Assistant transport, **eligible external-contract productions always rank ahead of eligible studio-original productions**. Only when no external-contract production is waiting for an eligible transport may an assistant select an original. Within the available priority class, use G-19's combined assistant-to-object distance, remaining deadline urgency where applicable, and assistant relationship with the professional who last worked on the production. Do not introduce progressive aging that overrides this strict class preference or an optional player-assigned deadline for original work. Player manual transport remains available, and an ineligible/blocked contract cannot block an otherwise eligible original.

Reservation and stage handoff must not produce duplicate transports or duplicate G-07 commits. Interruption recovery is resolved by G-22; manual player intervention is resolved by G-23; do not assume an implemented scheduler or validated Unreal runtime.

Recalculate G-19/G-20 priorities through G-21 reservation; do not cancel the project, discard content, re-charge creative production, or commit the next stage merely because of interruption. ## Production version, pre-release trash and published catalogue rules (updated 2026-11-16)

- **Three successfully generated versions total per creative stage**, not a rolling pool of three concurrent alternatives. After the third version, disable further new-version/rework requests for that stage. Selecting one version advances it and deletes all other versions from the stage.
- **G-07 accepted (2026-11-16): stage progression is irreversible.** A successful player-routed handoff to the next compatible workstation commits the selected/default result and permanently closes the preceding stage; no returning, revision or regeneration of closed stages, and no reopening spent creative slots. Only the current stage can offer its permitted alternatives before advancement. An incompatible/failed handoff does not advance or close anything. Persist this boundary and ignore stale AI results after stage closure.
- **Owner rule (2026-11-16) supersedes the prior post-release trash permission:** only **unreleased** in-production projects can be permanently trashed (delete project-owned drafts/versions/artifacts). **Spent money is not refunded**. Do not reintroduce a recoverable "cancelled project" archive history as a substitute for deletion.

## G-04 financial crisis and terminal company sale (accepted 2026-11-16)

- Financial distress **increases frequency of external offers while worsening their terms** through the normal contract system; it does not unlock an elaborate bankruptcy rescue menu.
- Unpaid employees **may** miss work, accept a rival's offer or take home a studio decorative object, according to game-owned state/personality; do not force universal employee misconduct.
- **G-04 owner refinement (2026-11-16) supersedes the earlier no-new-loan/permanent one-rescue rule.** At a qualifying negative-cash threshold crossing **without active emergency financing**, offer rescue that **settles old debts/arrears, leaves positive calculated cash buffer, and creates a separately tracked, repayable financing obligation**. Never describe the studio as free of all debt while this financing remains unpaid. Keep the old debt settlement and new financing balance separate; avoid double counting.
- A **second distinct** negative-threshold breach **while rescue remains unpaid** forces sale/game over. **Paying the rescue in full resets the crisis/eligibility condition**, permitting another rescue on a future eligible breach. Partial repayment is not enough. Do not increment the crossing count every tick/day of one continuous shortfall. Do not reverse historical people/asset consequences. **Repayment uses automatic scheduled instalments only**; no manual/early payment action. Make the schedule and remaining balance legible, and only full settlement resets insolvency eligibility. **Insufficient cash is not a separate nonpayment mechanic (accepted 2026-11-16):** charge each due instalment **in full even if cash becomes negative**, reduce financing balance, and apply the existing insolvency threshold. No special default fines, skipped-instalment state, dedicated nonpayment event or manual/prepayment flow. Ensure a final instalment reaching zero resets eligibility before classifying the resulting crisis, and avoid duplicate posting on save/reload. Amounts, cadence, duration, interest and numeric thresholds are not fixed.
- Financial game over presents a retrospective and authored/procedural **future epilogue for IPs, employees and rivals** grounded in persistent history; do not treat text generated by AI as authoritative world-state mutation.
- **G-04 final insolvency decision accepted (2026-11-16):** at the first eligible negative-cash crossing without unpaid rescue, **pause active simulation** for a mandatory **Accept / Decline financing Decision Event**. Accept applies old-debt settlement + positive cash buffer + separately tracked repayable financing **before resuming**; decline **immediately forces company sale and ends the campaign** in the retrospective/epilogue. No extra countdown, indefinite waiting below the threshold, repeated triggering while paused or rescue offer while previous financing is unpaid. A second distinct crossing while still unpaid forces sale directly; full payment re-enables the next eligible offer. Make terminal decline explicit; keep event/cycle consistent on save/reload. Threshold values, repayment terms, probabilities and Unreal runtime remain unvalidated.
